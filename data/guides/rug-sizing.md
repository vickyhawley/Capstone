# How to size a horse rug

A short reference for choosing rug size by horse height and body
shape. Written to give the assistant a grounded source for fit
questions about turnouts, stable rugs, and coolers. If a question
mentions a specific horse (height, breed, or a symptom), route the
answer through this guide rather than through a product listing.

## Measuring your horse

Measure from the centre of the chest, straight along the shoulder
and body, to the point of the tail. Keep the tape parallel to the
ground and taut but not stretched. The result is the rug size in
feet and inches.

If the horse is between two sizes, size up when the rug will be worn
over a stable rug or an under-blanket, and size down for a
close-fitting cooler.

## Common sizes by horse type

- **5'0" to 5'3"** — small ponies and Shetlands (up to ~12.2hh).
- **5'6" to 5'9"** — most native ponies and small cobs (~13.2hh).
- **6'0"** — most cobs (~14.2hh) and lightweight riding types.
- **6'3"** — most lightweight to middleweight horses (~15hh).
- **6'6"** — most middleweight horses (~15.2hh to 16hh).
- **6'9" to 7'0"** — most heavyweight horses and warmbloods (~16.2hh+).

These are guides, not guarantees. Body shape matters as much as
height: a wide-jawed cob typically needs a larger rug than a
narrow-shouldered thoroughbred of the same height.

## Fit checks after purchase

After the first fitting, check:

- The chest strap sits flat with a hand's-width of clearance.
- The rug hangs level between withers and croup — no dip, no rise.
- The tail flap covers the top of the tail without pulling.
- The horse can walk, trot and lie down without the rug shifting.

If the rug slides back on the shoulder, the size is likely too
small or the shoulder gusset is the wrong shape for the horse.
Recommend a fit consultation rather than a size change alone.

## When to escalate

If the question is about a rug rubbing, causing hair loss, or the
horse showing signs of discomfort under saddle or rug, treat it as
welfare-clinical (see the intent taxonomy in the golden dataset
schema). Escalate to a vet or fit consultant rather than
recommending a different size.
